Digital Tools for School Teachers 2026–27

Digital tools are most useful when they remove repeated work without creating a complicated process. A teacher can begin with a few small tasks: preparing a lesson plan, recording attendance, building a question paper, calculating marks and preparing a report. Choose tools that work on the devices already available to staff and students.

What to check before you choose

For each tool, check whether it works on a phone, whether it can print or save a PDF, and whether information is uploaded or stays in the browser. Do not enter unnecessary personal details about children into a tool. For official records, follow the school’s approved process and verify all output before issuing it.

Build a simple, reliable process

Introduce one tool at a time and ask teachers what saves time. Keep a clear fallback method for days when internet access is poor. A tool should make the task simpler, not force every teacher to change the entire way they work at once.
